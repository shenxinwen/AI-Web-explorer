\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

We design three controlled studies around the claims in
Sections~\ref{sec:problem-formulation} and~\ref{sec:method}. Rather than compare
end-to-end scores against systems with different tasks, action spaces,
executors, and label definitions, each study holds the relevant inputs fixed
and varies the mechanism under examination. We ask:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1:} Does evidence-grounded admission improve the precision
    of persistent functional knowledge while retaining human-supported claims?
    \item \textbf{RQ2:} Under a fixed ordinary-attempt budget, does persistent,
    execution-grounded exploration retain useful evidence-supported functional
    coverage, and what additional GUI cost does replay incur?
    \item \textbf{RQ3:} Can the risk assessor identify and type potential
    environmental consequences of selected actions, and how does current visual
    context affect its judgments?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Applications and Common Protocol}

RQ1 and RQ2 use two interactive shopping applications: SauceDemo and Practice
Shopping. Each exploration run starts from the designated entry URL in a clean
browser session; resettable application state is restored before a run.
Formal exploration uses three runs per application and condition, with at most
25 ordinary high-level candidate attempts per run. The budget counts attempts
to test candidate functions. Replay actions do not consume this budget because
they restore a previously observed context rather than test a new candidate;
we therefore limit and report their GUI actions separately.

For RQ1 and RQ2, all candidate hypotheses include an expected observable
outcome frozen before execution. The retained artifacts include the selected
action, executor status, before and after observations, evidence references,
and model judgments.
Initial annotations were generated with AI assistance and then reviewed and
revised item by item by a human author under a frozen annotation guide. The
reviewed labels are used as gold. This process is not independent double
annotation, so we do not report inter-annotator agreement. Failed executions,
incomplete evidence, and negative examples remain in the evaluation artifacts
rather than being discarded.

\subsection{RQ1: Evidence-Grounded Knowledge Admission}

RQ1 tests whether outcome evidence provides a more reliable admission boundary
than either proposal or executor status alone. The comparison is designed to
falsify this claim if evidence-grounded admission fails to improve precision or
does so only by discarding most human-supported knowledge.

\paragraph{Data and unit of analysis.}
RQ1 uses the naturally occurring candidates and frozen trajectories from three
full-method runs on each application. Duplicate attempts are aggregated by
application, semantic location, and canonical action identifier, yielding 45
unique functional claims backed by 103 attempts. Human review identifies a
claim as supported when at least one valid attempt both confirms that the
function exists at that location and provides before--after evidence for its
core functional outcome. The final set contains 33 supported claims. Four of
the 103 attempts lack complete evidence and remain non-admissible under the
evidence-grounded rule.

\paragraph{Compared admission rules.}
All three rules consume exactly the same candidates and interaction traces:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Proposal-as-fact} admits every proposed claim;
    \item \textbf{Executor-success-as-fact} admits a claim after at least one
    attempt is reported successful by the executor;
    \item \textbf{Evidence-grounded admission} admits a claim only after at
    least one evidence-complete attempt is judged to support its frozen
    expected outcome.
\end{itemize}
This comparison isolates the admission rule from differences in exploration or
execution.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
The primary measures are admitted knowledge precision, the fraction of admitted
claims supported by human review; supported knowledge retention, the fraction
of human-supported claims admitted; and admission yield, the fraction of all
candidates admitted. We additionally inspect false admissions, missed supported
claims, evidence completeness, and disagreements between executor status and
functional outcome.

\subsection{RQ2: Persistent Evidence Production}

RQ2 tests whether preserving and recovering unresolved verification
opportunities produces additional evidence-supported functionality under the
same ordinary candidate-attempt budget. The relevant comparison is Full versus
Linear: both use the same dependency-aware selection and admission rule, while
only Full retains a frontier across locations and replays observed paths. Any
coverage gain must therefore be interpreted together with replay's additional
GUI cost.

\paragraph{Conditions.}
We compare three exploration policies while holding the applications, entry
states, ordinary-attempt budget, candidate representation, executor, and
evidence admission rule fixed. \textbf{Random} selects among unfinished
candidates using a frozen seed, without checking observed dependencies or
replaying prior paths. \textbf{Linear} checks observed direct dependencies and
uses deterministic selection, but abandons unresolved candidates after leaving
their semantic location. \textbf{Full} adds a persistent frontier and replay to
Linear, allowing the explorer to return to an observed location and continue
evidence production. The design comprises 18 valid runs: two applications,
three conditions, and three repetitions.

\paragraph{Coverage gold and metrics.}
The manually reviewed coverage inventories contain 16 functions for Practice
Shopping and 22 for SauceDemo. Within a run, a listed function counts as
covered at most once and only when an evidence-complete interaction supports
its functional outcome. The primary metric is evidence-supported functional
coverage, reported as both a count and a percentage for each run, averaged
within each application and then macro-averaged across applications. Coverage
growth over ordinary attempts is descriptive. We separately report ordinary
attempt counts, execution outcomes, stopping reasons, the number of replay
operations and GUI actions, and functions first covered after replay. Because
replay has an additional GUI cost, higher coverage under the ordinary-attempt
budget is not interpreted as higher total-interaction efficiency.

\subsection{RQ3: Context-Conditioned Environmental Risk Awareness}

RQ3 tests whether a pre-execution risk record can be produced for VERA's own
selected actions and whether visual context improves the grounding of risk
judgments. No single dataset adequately answers both questions: naturally
occurring exploration actions provide workflow fidelity but a narrow risk
distribution, whereas a deliberately varied challenge set supplies stronger
contextual contrasts but not actions proposed by VERA. We therefore use two
complementary tracks and report them separately.

The
\textbf{exploration-linked track} contains 106 run-locally deduplicated ordinary
actions selected during the six valid Full runs from RQ2. Human gold contains
32 risk-positive actions, drawn from the financial-transaction and
sensitive-data categories. We evaluate four inputs to the same risk judge:
action label only; action label with the taxonomy; screenshot with the action
label; and the full screenshot, action label, and taxonomy input. This track
tests whether risk records can be produced for actions arising in VERA's own
exploration workflow. Replay is retained for audit but excluded from this
classification comparison.

The \textbf{external context-challenge track} contains 100 independently
sampled screenshot--action cases, including 30 candidate context pairs and 40
additional diversity samples. The actions are supplied to the assessor; this
track does not claim that VERA's explorer would propose them. Human review
labels 65 cases as risk-positive and permits multiple acceptable risk types for
compound cases, while the assessor still returns one primary type. We compare
\textbf{Text-only} (action label and the shared taxonomy) with
\textbf{Context-conditioned} (screenshot, action label, and the same taxonomy),
thereby isolating the contribution of visual context. The taxonomy is shared
output vocabulary, not a separately evaluated predictor.

Both tracks report binary precision, recall, and F1, plus risk-type accuracy on
human-positive cases. For the external track, a predicted type is correct if it
matches any human-accepted type. We also analyze context-corrected decisions,
new errors, false positives, false negatives, and context-pair consistency.
External uncertainty estimates use 10,000 cluster-bootstrap samples, treating
each candidate pair as one cluster and each diversity example as a singleton
(seed 20260920). Differences whose intervals cross zero are described only as
point-estimate directions. The assessed risks are recorded before execution;
the experiments do not change actions or evaluate an intervention policy.
