\section{VERA}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Design Principles and Overview}

VERA treats open-ended functional model induction as evidence production under
two linked risks. A proposed function or executor-reported completion may not
establish the expected application-level outcome, while the interaction used
to obtain that evidence can itself alter the environment. The method therefore
governs both how claims become persistent knowledge and how their acquisition
actions are represented.

Three principles follow. First, \emph{claims precede evidence}: the expected
observable outcome is fixed before execution. Second, \emph{knowledge requires
outcome evidence}: executor status is diagnostic, whereas admission depends on
support for the frozen outcome. Third, \emph{evidence acquisition is traceable
interaction}: the selected action receives a contextual risk record linked to
the claim, execution, and outcome evidence. VERA operationalizes these
principles in a persistent loop that proposes claims, records pre-action risk,
collects evidence, judges outcomes, and preserves unresolved verification
opportunities. Its generators, executor, and judges are replaceable; the method
is the protocol connecting their outputs into $\mathcal{M}$.

\subsection{From Functional Claim to Knowledge Admission}

At semantic location $\ell$, the proposal component conditions on the current
GUI and accumulated model to specify a high-level function and expected
observable outcome. Proposals describe application-level functionality---such
as adding an item or creating a project---rather than treating every widget as
a separate function. The expectation may allow equivalent visible
manifestations but cannot rely only on hidden backend state. VERA stores it
before interaction and does not revise it after observing the result; the new
hypothesis initially remains unresolved.

The browser executor receives the selected semantic action. VERA retains the
pre-execution observation, concrete interaction trace, executor status,
post-execution observation, URLs, and available structured changes or evidence
references. Preserving both status and outcome evidence is essential: a click
or input sequence can complete without producing the intended application
effect, while an execution failure can reflect grounding or timing rather than
the absence of the function.

The outcome verifier compares this packet with the frozen claim and assigns
\textsc{Supported}, \textsc{Unsupported}, or \textsc{Unresolved}. VERA then
applies Equation~\ref{eq:admission}; only complete, supporting evidence produces
admitted knowledge. The current implementation uses a vision-language model,
but the protocol is verifier-independent. Non-admitted claims remain linked to
their evidence and diagnostic reasons so that uncertainty is inspectable and
can become a future verification opportunity.

VERA separately records location constraints and observed direct dependencies.
These metadata help select hypotheses that are executable in the current
context, but do not change the outcome claim or assert complete business
preconditions.

\subsection{Pre-Execution Risk Awareness}

Evidence acquisition is an environmental interaction, not a neutral internal
operation. Before each selected action, VERA combines its semantic label with
the current screenshot and a versioned risk taxonomy. An independent
vision-language judge returns a binary \texttt{potential\_risk} decision, one
primary \texttt{risk\_type} when applicable, and brief interface-grounded
evidence. The taxonomy supplies shared category definitions and examples rather
than acting as a predictor itself.

Because risk is conditioned on the current GUI, identical action labels can
receive different judgments in different states. VERA links each record to the
assessed action and, for ordinary verification, to the claim and later outcome
evidence; replay steps are assessed separately at the same semantic-action
granularity. A downstream policy could consume this signal for blocking or
human confirmation. In the evaluated system, however, the record does not
alter the selected action, and a risk-inference error is retained while
execution continues.

\subsection{Persistent Evidence Production and Traceability}

Conservative admission creates unresolved claims whenever interaction leaves a
semantic location, execution fails, or the current budget ends. VERA retains
these claims in a persistent frontier rather than equating interruption with
rejection. Selection prioritizes hypotheses whose observed direct dependencies
are satisfied. When an unresolved claim remains at a previously visited
location, replay can restore an observed path and resume evidence production;
it does not relax the evidence or admission rule.

Replay is additional interaction, so VERA records its operations, GUI actions,
and risk annotations separately from ordinary candidate attempts. This makes it
possible to hold the candidate-attempt budget fixed while reporting the cost
and supported functionality obtained after recovery.

The resulting model links, for each claim, its frozen expectation, selected
action, pre-execution risk record, interaction trace, before--after evidence,
judgment, and admission decision. Supported, unsupported, and unresolved claims
remain distinguishable. Downstream consumers may use a conservative view of
admitted functions and supported outcomes, while the complete trace remains
available for inspection or further verification.
